# Vannevar Bush

## Overview
**Dr. Vannevar Bush** (1890–1974) was an American engineer, inventor, and science administrator who headed the U.S. Office of Scientific Research and Development (OSRD) during World War II. He is universally recognized as one of the conceptual founding fathers of hypertext, information retrieval, and cognitive computing.

## Key Contributions
- **"As We May Think" (1945):** Authored the visionary essay outlining the information overload crisis and introducing the [[memex|Memex]] concept.
- **Associative Indexing:** Formulated the theoretical foundation that human memory and intellect are augmented through networks of associative connections rather than rigid hierarchical files.
- **Inspiration for Modern Computing:** His ideas directly inspired Douglas Engelbart (inventor of the mouse and NLS), Ted Nelson (coiner of "hypertext" and Project Xanadu), and Tim Berners-Lee (World Wide Web).

## Role in the LLM Wiki Ecosystem
- Bush's vision of an "intimate supplement to memory" with associative trails is the philosophical blueprint for personal knowledge bases visualized in [[obsidian]] and maintained by LLM agents.
- His "trail blazers" concept is automated in the LLM Wiki architecture, where LLM agents build and maintain trails without human friction.
