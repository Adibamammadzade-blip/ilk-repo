# Memex

## Overview
The **Memex** (a portmanteau of "memory" and "index" or "extender") is a hypothetical electro-mechanical device conceived by [[vannevar-bush|Vannevar Bush]] in 1945. It was designed as an intimate personal desk and filing system to store an individual's books, records, and communications on high-density microfilm.

## Mechanical & Architectural Features
- **Physical Desk Interface:** Fitted with dual translucent viewing screens, keyboard, buttons, and motorized levers for rapid traversal.
- **High-Density Storage:** Used miniaturized micro-photographic records capable of holding millions of pages in a fraction of desk space.
- **Direct Dual-Screen Linking:** Allowed a user to project two separate documents simultaneously and bind them together with a permanent code key.
- **Associative Trails:** Enabled continuous, non-linear sequences of related documents with user-annotated side branches (`[[associative-trails]]`).

## Historical Significance
While never physically built with 1940s vacuum tubes and microfilm, Memex became the primary conceptual catalyst for:
1. **Hypertext & The World Wide Web:** Direct precursor to the HTML hyperlink and HTTP web graph.
2. **Personal Knowledge Management (PKM):** The precursor to Zettelkasten card files and digital tools like [[obsidian]].
3. **LLM Wiki Pattern:** The transition from manual electro-mechanical indexing to autonomous semantic compilation by LLM agents.
