# Source: As We May Think (1945)

## Summary
In this seminal 1945 essay published in *The Atlantic Monthly*, Dr. Vannevar Bush addressed the post-WWII explosion of scientific literature and the fatal flaw of artificial hierarchical indexing (alphabetical/numerical filing). He proposed the **Memex**—a mechanized desk and personal file repository based on **associative indexing**, where items are tied together into persistent **associative trails**. This paper served as the direct conceptual ancestor of modern hypertext, personal knowledge management (PKM), the Web, and the [[llm-wiki-concept|LLM Wiki]].

## Key Claims & Contributions
1. **The Indexing Dilemma:** Hierarchical and alphabetical indexing forces information into singular, artificial paths. The human brain, conversely, functions by *association*—snapping rapidly across interconnected mental trails.
2. **The Memex Device:** Proposed an electro-mechanical desk storing books, records, and personal communications on microfilm, equipped with translucent screens and rapid recall levers.
3. **Associative Trails:** The core mechanic where any two items can be permanently linked together. Trails can branch into side-trails, merge into master sequences, and never fade over time (`[[associative-trails]]`).
4. **Professional Trail Blazers:** Envisioned an emerging profession of people who curate and build useful trails through the common record.
5. **Cognitive Extension:** The true goal of externalized memory systems is not mere archival storage, but augmenting the human intellect and coordinating accumulated wisdom.
