# Source: Augmenting Human Intellect (Engelbart, 1962)

## Summary
In this groundbreaking 1962 SRI report, Dr. Douglas Engelbart formalized the conceptual framework for augmenting human cognitive capability through external symbol manipulation systems. He introduced the **H-LAM/T system** (Human using Language, Artifacts, Methodology, and Training) and the principle of **bootstrapping** (using current cognitive tools to design better future tools). This work directly realized [[vannevar-bush|Vannevar Bush]]'s [[memex|Memex]] and laid the theoretical foundation for interactive computing, the mouse, hypertext, and modern AI co-intelligence systems.

## Key Claims & Contributions
1. **Definition of Intellectual Augmentation:** Increasing human capacity to approach complex problems, gain rapid comprehension, and derive creative solutions beyond natural biological working memory bounds.
2. **The H-LAM/T System:** Human intellect operates as an integrated system across four pillars: Language (conceptual models), Artifacts (tools/storage), Methodology (strategies/protocols), and Training (skills) (`[[h-lam-t-system]]`).
3. **External Symbol Manipulation:** Human working memory is strictly bounded ($7 \pm 2$ items). Augmenting intellect requires externalizing thoughts onto interactive artifacts capable of multi-dimensional linking and traversal.
4. **Bootstrapping Principle:** Enhancing the core tools of knowledge curation accelerates all subsequent scientific and analytical output in a compounding feedback loop.
5. **Human-Machine Symbiosis:** The computer is not an autonomous replacement for human judgment, but an intimate cognitive partner executing formal retrieval and consistency checks while the human directs inquiry.
